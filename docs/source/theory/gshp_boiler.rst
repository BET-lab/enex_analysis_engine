Ground Source Heat Pump Boiler (GSHPB)
======================================

.. seealso::
   
   API 레퍼런스: :mod:`enex_analysis.ground_source_heat_pump_boiler`

System Overview
---------------

The Ground Source Heat Pump Boiler (GSHPB) couples a vapour-compression refrigerant cycle with a Borehole Heat Exchanger (BHE) on the evaporator side and a thermal storage tank on the condenser side. The model dynamically resolves the refrigerant cycle, finding the optimal operating point at each time step. Concurrently, the long-term ground thermal response is evaluated using spatial-superposition g-functions to accurately capture borehole thermal interference and temperature drift over multi-year simulations.

Modeling Assumptions
--------------------

- **Lumped Thermal Capacitance:** The thermal storage tank water is fully mixed and represented by a single uniform temperature.
- **Analytical Condenser:** Condenser approach temperature is solved analytically based on required heat load and constant UA.
- **Isenthalpic Expansion:** The expansion process through the expansion valve is considered perfectly isenthalpic.
- **BHE Circulation Pump:** The circulation pump operates at a constant power (:math:`E_\text{pmp}`) and flow rate during active heating cycles, adding heat to the evaporator inlet fluid.
- **Ground Thermal Response:** Long-term thermal drift and short-term transient responses are calculated using precomputed step-response g-functions.

Mathematical Modeling
---------------------

Refrigerant Cycle and Heat Exchangers
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Unlike air-source systems, the GSHPB evaporator is coupled to the fluid circulating through the BHE. The fluid entering the evaporator (:math:`T_\text{evap,in}`) absorbs the heat generated by the circulation pump (:math:`E_\text{pmp}`):

.. math::

   T_\text{evap,in} = T_\text{bhe,f,out} + \frac{E_\text{pmp}}{\dot{m}_\text{bhe} \cdot c_{p,\text{w}}}

The evaporating saturation temperature is then determined by the approach temperature difference (:math:`\Delta T_\text{ref,evap}`):

.. math::

   T_\text{ref,evap,sat} = T_\text{evap,in} - \Delta T_\text{ref,evap}

The condensing saturation temperature (:math:`T_\text{ref,cond,sat}`) is anchored to the tank water temperature (:math:`T_\text{w,tank}`) using the condenser approach temperature, directly proportional to the heating load (:math:`Q_\text{ref,cond}`) and its thermal conductance (:math:`UA_\text{cond}`):

.. math::

   \Delta T_\text{ref,cond} &= \frac{Q_\text{ref,cond}}{UA_\text{cond}} \\
   T_\text{ref,cond,sat} &= T_\text{w,tank} + \Delta T_\text{ref,cond}

The compressor and expansion valve inlet states are calculated using user-specified superheat (:math:`\Delta T_\text{superheat}`) and subcool (:math:`\Delta T_\text{subcool}`) margins:

.. math::

   T_\text{ref,cmp,in} &= T_\text{ref,evap,sat} + \Delta T_\text{superheat} \\
   T_\text{ref,exp,in} &= T_\text{ref,cond,sat} - \Delta T_\text{subcool}

Energy Balance and Performance
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The required refrigerant mass flow rate (:math:`\dot{m}_\text{ref}`) is determined by the target condenser load and the enthalpy difference:

.. math::

   \dot{m}_\text{ref} = \frac{Q_\text{ref,cond}}{h_\text{ref,cmp,out} - h_\text{ref,exp,in}}

The evaporator heat absorption rate (:math:`Q_\text{ref,evap}`) and the compressor electrical power input (:math:`E_\text{cmp}`) are computed as:

.. math::

   Q_\text{ref,evap} &= \dot{m}_\text{ref} (h_\text{ref,cmp,in} - h_\text{ref,exp,out}) \\
   E_\text{cmp} &= \dot{m}_\text{ref} (h_\text{ref,cmp,out} - h_\text{ref,cmp,in})

Borehole Heat Exchanger (BHE) Model
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The actual heat extracted from the ground (:math:`Q_\text{bhe}`) accounts for the heat added by the circulation pump:

.. math::

   Q_\text{bhe} = Q_\text{ref,evap} - E_\text{pmp}

The transient temperature change at the borehole wall (:math:`\Delta T_\text{bhe}`) is evaluated using the temporal superposition of step heat extraction pulses and the spatial-response g-function (:math:`g(t)`):

.. math::

   T_\text{bhe}(t) = T_s - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{q_i - q_{i-1}}{2 \pi k_s} g(t_n - t_{i-1})

Where:
- :math:`T_s` is the undisturbed ground temperature.
- :math:`q_i` is the specific heat extraction rate per unit borehole length at time step :math:`i`.
- :math:`k_s` is the soil thermal conductivity.

The average circulating fluid temperature (:math:`T_\text{bhe,f}`) is subsequently derived using the effective borehole thermal resistance (:math:`R_b`):

.. math::

   T_\text{bhe,f} = T_\text{bhe} - q \cdot R_b

Optimal Operating Point
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

At each simulation time step, the model identifies the optimal evaporation approach temperature (:math:`\Delta T_\text{ref,evap}`) that satisfies the physical NTU limits of the evaporator while minimizing the total electrical power input (:math:`E_\text{tot}`). The total power includes both the compressor and the BHE circulation pump:

.. math::

   \min_{\Delta T_\text{ref,evap}} \quad E_\text{tot} = E_\text{cmp} + E_\text{pmp}

This optimization is solved via 1-D Brent's method.
